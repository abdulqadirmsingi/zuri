Use `Accordion` for content people scan and open one piece at a time, such as frequently asked questions. Don't hide anything essential inside one.

## Installation

<InstallTabs name="accordion" />

This adds `@radix-ui/react-accordion` and two small keyframes (`zuri-accordion-down` and `-up`) to your CSS, which animate the height.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Accordion, AccordionContent, AccordionItem, AccordionTrigger } from "@/components/ui/accordion";

<Accordion type="single" collapsible>
  <AccordionItem value="fees">
    <AccordionTrigger>What are the fees?</AccordionTrigger>
    <AccordionContent>Sending to another wallet is free.</AccordionContent>
  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>
```

`type="single"` keeps one section open (add `collapsible` so it can also be closed), and `type="multiple"` lets several stay open.

## Accessibility

- Each trigger is a real `<button>` inside a heading with `aria-expanded`, and the open panel is a `region` named by its trigger.
- Enter and Space toggle a section. Arrow Down and Up move between triggers, Home and End jump to the first and last, and disabled sections are skipped.
- The chevron is decorative and hidden from screen readers; the state is announced through `aria-expanded`.
- The height animates using the `--duration-base` token, which is almost zero under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Each trigger is at least 44 pixels tall.

## Tokens used

`--border`, `--muted-foreground`, `--ring`, `--duration-base`, `--ease-out`.

## Known limitations

- The animation changes the panel's height, which is layout. That is cheap for short text but avoid very large panels.
- If you copy the component by hand, remember the two keyframes, or the panels open without animation.
