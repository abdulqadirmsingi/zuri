Use `CardFanHero` to show off a handful of things, such as products, collections or categories, under a centred headline. The cards overlap and tilt away from the middle one, and each lifts a little on hover. Its width adapts to the number of cards, so the fan fits down to 320px.

## Usage

```tsx
import { CardFanHero } from "@/components/ui/card-fan-hero";

<CardFanHero
  title="Everything you need, in one place"
  actions={<a href="/browse">Browse all</a>}
  cards={[
    { id: "maps", label: "Maps", media: <img src="/maps.jpg" alt="" className="size-full object-cover" /> },
    // three to five cards work best
  ]}
/>
```

## How the fan is laid out

Each card turns 5 degrees more than the one before it, away from the middle, and drops a little on a curve. The middle cards sit on top. The cards are a plain list of figures, so a screen reader reads them in order.

## Props

It extends the native `<section>` props (except `title`) and spreads them onto the section, including `ref` (React 19). It is a Server Component: it has no state and needs no JavaScript.

## Accessibility

- The section is a landmark named by its headline (`aria-labelledby`). The headline is an `h1` by default; pass `headingLevel={2}` if the page already has one.
- Text never sits on top of an image, so its contrast does not depend on the picture you choose.
- Give each image its own `alt` text, or `alt=""` if it is purely decorative.
- Make the buttons or links you pass as `actions` at least 24px tall (these examples use 44px).

## Tokens used

`--background`, `--border`, `--muted`, `--accent`, `--duration-base`, `--ease-out`. Under reduced motion the hover lift is removed.

## Known limitations

- It lays out the content you give it; it does not choose or crop images for you. Use a ratio-friendly image, and `object-cover` on it.
- The demos use plain shapes as stand-in artwork. Bring your own pictures.
